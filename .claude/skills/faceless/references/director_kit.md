# Faceless Director Kit — 9:16, 30-60s, voiceover-led B-roll (Veo / Kling / Seedance)

## 1. Hook formula — first 2 seconds [social-hook, cinematic]
- 0.0-0.3s GRAB: one stimulus only (visual shock, sound hit or pattern break). Never open on a slow establishing shot [cinematic: Mistake 1].
- 0.3-0.8s GAP: show incomplete information; the VO line asks or implies a question.
- 0.8-1.5s PROOF: confirm the hook is real, energy rises.
- 1.5-2.0s COMMIT: deliver a piece of the promise, pull into the story.
- Stack 2-3 hooks: e.g. silence -> sound hit + extreme macro -> wide reveal [cinematic: Hook Stacking].
- Best faceless hooks: extreme macro of a familiar object, black-to-light burst, reverse motion, tiny figure in a vast space, cold-to-gold color snap [cinematic: Hook Table].
- The VO's first sentence = a number, a contradiction or a stake. The picture shows it, not just says it.

## 2. Beat structure (45s default) [social-hook: Curiosity Ladder; brand-story: Master Template]
| Time | Beat | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2s | Hook | Stop the scroll |
| 2-5s | Confirm | Prove the hook is real, add a little more |
| 5-10s | Mini-cliff | New question, first pattern break [social-hook] |
| 10-20s | Story / proof | Your experience: the moment it changed (inciting incident) [brand-story] |
| 20-35s | Payoff | The lesson / reveal, emotional peak, second pattern break |
| 35-45s | Landing | Return to the opening image, transformed; quiet CTA, no hard sell [brand-story] |
- Pattern break about every 5s: new angle, new light, new sound [social-hook].
- Movement pace: slow 0-5s, faster 5-30s, slow at the landing [cinematic: Pacing Velocity].

## 3. Script -> 5-8 shot prompts
1. Split the VO into sentences. One shot = one idea = 3-8s of VO. Aim for 6 shots in 45s.
2. Tag each sentence with a beat (Hook/Confirm/Cliff/Story/Payoff/Landing).
3. Pick a visual metaphor per shot, not a literal picture: light/dark = clarity, path = journey, hands shaping = craft, seed/growth = compounding [brand-story: Visual Metaphor Library].
4. Vary the shot SIZE and MOVE every shot (wide -> macro -> medium -> top-down...). Never two in a row with the same move [cinematic: Mistake 2].
5. Lock one look for the whole video (same grade + grain + lens) so the shots cut as one film.
6. Max 3 characters per shot; name every object; actions must be physically logical [realism playbook].
7. Generate 480p/draft first, then final; 24fps [realism playbook].

### Shot prompt template
```
[IDENTITY LOCK if a person/character recurs: "Same man as reference, do not recast, do not beautify."]
[SHOT SIZE + SUBJECT + ACTION]: Extreme close-up of ___ doing ___.
[WORLD, every object named]: ___, ___, ___, ___.
[CAMERA MOVE + SPEED]: slow dolly-in, 2 ft/s, focus locked on ___.
[LENS]: 35mm / 50mm / 85mm, f/1.8 shallow depth.
[LIGHT]: key ___ at 45 deg, fill ratio 3:1, rim ___, color temp ___K.
[ATMOSPHERE]: haze / dust motes / rain / steam.
[SOUND CUE]: ambient ___, foley ___, one hit at ___s.
[GRADE]: ___.
[FORMAT]: 9:16 vertical, 5-8s, 24fps, no text, no logos.
[REALISM]: Shot on 35mm at f/1.8, unretouched skin with visible pores, real fabric texture, fine film grain, no plastic skin.
```
Rule: write the camera, speed and focus as numbers. "Dolly forward 3 ft at 2 ft/s" beats "camera moves forward" [cinematic: Mistakes 2, 4, 8].

## 4. Camera move library (15)
1. Push-in / dolly forward, 2 ft/s — intimacy, tension [cinematic]
2. Pull-back reveal — context, "oh wow" scale [cinematic]
3. Whip pan, 0.5s — energy, hides a cut [cinematic]
4. Parallax layer move — depth, premium feel [cinematic]
5. Handheld micro-jitter — realism, urgency, documentary [cinematic]
6. Tracking / follow cam from the side — subject in motion [cinematic]
7. Crane up / aerial rise — majesty, ending shots [cinematic]
8. Orbit 270 deg at constant distance — hero moment [cinematic]
9. Rack focus foreground -> background, 1.5s — redirect attention [cinematic]
10. Dutch angle 20-25 deg — unease, "something is wrong" [cinematic, fight-scenes]
11. Extreme close-up insert — detail, texture, macro-as-landscape [cinematic]
12. Top-down / bird's eye — layout, desk, money, plans [cinematic]
13. POV first-person walk — immersion [cinematic]
14. Drone approach down-and-in — establishing a place [real-estate]
15. Low worm's-eye angle + snap zoom — power, impact beat [fight-scenes]
Bonus: gimbal lateral glide (pure sideways, no pan) for calm luxury [real-estate].

## 5. Lighting library (10) [cinematic: Lighting Theory]
1. Three-point: warm key 45 deg, fill 3:1, rim behind — clean authority
2. Chiaroscuro: 90% shadow, one hard source — mystery, truth, noir
3. Silhouette backlight — power, anonymity (perfect for faceless)
4. Golden hour: 15 deg sun, 3200K — hope, beginnings, endings
5. Moonlight: cool 6000K+ top light — night, isolation, reflection
6. Practical neon / screens in frame — city, tech, hustle
7. Soft overcast — calm, honest, teaching moments
8. Volumetric god rays + dust motes — faith, revelation [also brand-story: Light/Dark metaphor]
9. Fluorescent 4500K green-grey — the 9-to-5 trap, institutions
10. Firelight / candle, 2000K flicker — intimacy, prayer, late-night study
Automotive/product beat: hard key 45 deg above, 40% fill, strong rim, dark reflective floor [product-360].

## 6. Grade library (8) [cinematic: Color Grading Presets]
1. Teal & orange — modern, high stakes
2. Gold & black (warm 3200K, crushed blacks) — premium brand look
3. Desaturated + one accent color at 100% — symbolic focus (e.g. only gold stays)
4. Cool blue 6500K — before/struggle beats
5. Bleach bypass, lifted blacks, grain — gritty truth
6. Vintage film stock, 70% saturation — memory, backstory
7. High-contrast B&W — timeless statements
8. Warm-cool split (warm subject, cool background) — conflict, duality
Keep saturation 100-120%, never 150%+ [cinematic: Mistake 7]. Cold grade for the problem, gold for the answer = the story told in color [cinematic: Extreme Color Shift].

## 7. Sound design [cinematic: Sound Design Guide; social-hook: Sound Strategy]
- Layers: ambient bed (-4dB) / foley synced to action (0dB) / music (-2 to -4dB under VO) / impact hits (+1 to +3dB).
- VO is the reference level; music ducks under it.
- Silence 1-2s then a hit = the strongest hook move; never leave silence without a payoff.
- Cut on sound, not on silence; land key visual changes on a beat.
- Sound sells texture: sizzle = heat, pour = abundance, crunch = quality [food-beverage].
- Original voice + licensed bed beats trending audio for a brand channel (trending = reach, not identity) [social-hook].
- Small rooms = short reverb, big spaces = long tails [cinematic].

## 8. Staying monetized — avoid YouTube "mass-produced / repetitive content"
- Own angle: every video carries a point of view only you have (Uber driver who trades, faith + business). No generic "10 facts" lists.
- Own voice: script written by you from your experience; real recorded voice preferred, or a consistent voice clone of YOU. Never the same stock TTS reading reworded articles.
- Own story: at least one personal beat per video (a ride, a trade, a verse that changed a decision) [brand-story: Origin / Day-in-the-Life arcs].
- Varied visuals: new shots generated per script; no reusing the same 10 clips across uploads; rotate camera moves, lighting and settings; vary shot size every cut.
- Varied format: alternate structures (story, breakdown, myth vs truth, day-in-the-life) so uploads do not share one template.
- Add value the B-roll cannot: commentary, a specific lesson, numbers, a takeaway.
- Mix in real footage (car dashcam, chart screen recordings, hands) between AI shots — proof of a human creator.
- Disclose realistic AI content where YouTube's altered-content label applies.
- Cadence over volume: fewer, distinct videos beat many near-duplicates.
